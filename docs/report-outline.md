# Final report outline and evidence map

## Required framing

The report is the graded deliverable. It must describe the implementation honestly, cite only verifiable sources, link the repository as reproducibility evidence, and include a brief explanatory statement connecting the work to PHY4000. The instructor specifically requested that cosmological significance remain visible alongside the machine-learning work.

## Recommended structure

1. **Title and abstract** — state the two-stage false-positive-reduction question, the frozen TOI snapshot, evaluation design, and main test-set result.
2. **Cosmological context** — explain how transiting-exoplanet detections and reliable false-positive handling affect what can be inferred about the prevalence of planetary systems. Cite Ricker et al. (2015), Sullivan et al. (2015), and the TESS context.
3. **Data and provenance** — state the NASA query, retrieval date, label rule, excluded dispositions, SPOC 120-second constraint, eligibility counts, and temporal TIC-grouped split. Cite the Archive and Guerrero et al. (2021).
4. **Methods** — describe quality filtering, detrending, BLS, global/local views, Stage 1, Stage 2 diagnostics, validation-only thresholding, and leakage controls. Cite Kovács et al. (2002), Lightkurve, Shallue & Vanderburg (2018), and ExoMiner as appropriate.
5. **Results** — include split table, baseline metrics, Stage 1 metrics, Stage 2 ablation table, ROC/PR curves, confusion matrices, operating point, and TIC-grouped bootstrap interval for Stage 2 minus Stage 1 false-positive rate.
6. **Error analysis and limitations** — show representative CP/FP/FA curves, identify failures, distinguish the frozen supervised test from a live demo, and discuss cadence, availability, labels, domain shift, and selection effects.
7. **Discussion and explanatory statement** — interpret the result without claiming discovery/validation of individual planets; tie robust candidate rejection to occurrence/population studies and PHY4000 themes.
8. **References and reproducibility** — verify each bibliography entry against its original source and provide the public repository URL and run/dataset manifest references.

## Do-not-claim list

- Do not call a high model score a planet discovery or validation.
- Do not report a test metric selected through test-set tuning.
- Do not use catalogue period/midpoint as a formal model feature.
- Do not treat several sector observations of a TIC as independent samples.
- Do not omit failed downloads, unavailable cadence, or excluded records from the dataset flow.
